    Table 1: Symptoms & Signs (NICE NG224, 2022)

    Use alongside clinical judgement to decide whether urine collection and testing is necessary (Recs 1.1.1–1.1.4). Do not routinely test urine in children ≥3 months with clear signs of an alternative infection unless they remain unwell with diagnostic uncertainty.

    Increases Likelihood of UTI

    • Painful urination (dysuria)
    • More frequent urination
    • New bedwetting
    • Foul-smelling (malodorous) urine
    • Darker urine
    • Cloudy urine
    • Frank haematuria (visible blood)
    • Reduced fluid intake
    • Fever
    • Shivering
    • Abdominal pain
    • Loin or suprapubic tenderness
    • Capillary refill >3 seconds
    • Previous confirmed UTI

    Decreases Likelihood of UTI

    • Absence of painful urination (dysuria)
    • Nappy rash
    • Breathing difficulties
    • Abnormal chest sounds
    • Abnormal ear examination
    • Fever with known alternative cause
    Upper vs Lower UTI (Recs 1.1.25–1.1.27):
    • Acute upper UTI (pyelonephritis): Bacteriuria + fever ≥38°C, OR bacteriuria + fever <38°C with loin pain/tenderness.
    • Lower UTI (cystitis): Bacteriuria with no systemic symptoms/signs.
    • Do not use CRP alone to differentiate upper from lower UTI.

    Urine Collection & Preservation (Recs 1.1.11–1.1.18)

    1
    Sample Before Antibiotics (Recs 1.1.11–1.1.12)

    Obtain urine before giving antibiotics. In high risk of serious illness, do not delay treatment if a sample cannot be obtained.

    2
    Preferred Non-Invasive Collection (Recs 1.1.13–1.1.14)

    Use clean catch wherever possible. If not possible, use manufacturer-approved urine collection pads.

    !
    Prohibited Collection Materials (Rec 1.1.15)

    Do NOT use cotton wool balls, gauze, or sanitary towels to collect urine from babies and children.

    3
    Invasive Sampling (Rec 1.1.16)

    Use catheter samples or ultrasound-guided suprapubic aspiration (SPA) when non-invasive collection is not possible or practical.

    4
    Preservation & Boric Acid Fill-Line Rule (Recs 1.1.17–1.1.18)

    If culture cannot occur within 4 hours, refrigerate immediately or use boric acid. Follow manufacturer fill-line instructions strictly—underfilling causes toxic boric acid concentrations that kill uropathogens (false negative).

    Table 2: Dipstick Strategy for Children ≥3 Years (Rec 1.1.20)

    Dipstick Result NICE NG224 Strategy
    LE + and Nitrite + Assume UTI and give antibiotics. Send urine for culture if high/intermediate risk of serious illness or history of previous UTI.
    LE − and Nitrite + Give antibiotics if tested on a fresh urine sample. Send urine for culture; adjust management on culture result.
    LE + and Nitrite − Send urine for microscopy & culture. Do not give antibiotics unless good clinical evidence of UTI (e.g. obvious urinary symptoms). Consider extra-urinary infection.
    LE − and Nitrite − Assume no UTI. Do not give UTI antibiotics or send culture (unless Rec 1.1.21 applies). Explore other causes.

    Table 3: Interpreting Microscopy Results (Rec 1.1.22)

    Microscopy Finding Interpretation
    Pyuria + and Bacteriuria + Assume the baby or child has a UTI.
    Pyuria + and Bacteriuria − Start antibiotic treatment if the baby or child has symptoms or signs of a UTI.
    Pyuria − and Bacteriuria + Assume the baby or child has a UTI.
    Pyuria − and Bacteriuria − Assume the baby or child does not have a UTI (unless clinical criteria strongly suggest false negative, Rec 1.1.23).
